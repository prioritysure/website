Navigating the Complex World of Chiropractic Reimbursement

Chiropractic practices face unique billing challenges under Medicare and commercial insurance guidelines. Medicare strictly limits coverage to manual manipulation of the spine to correct a subluxation (CPT 98940–98942) and requires precise linkage between primary subluxation codes (M99.00–M99.05) and secondary neuromuscular condition codes. Additionally, the **AT modifier** (Acute Treatment) must be applied accurately to distinguish active care from non-covered maintenance care.

Commercial carriers often bundle physical modalities (such as electrical stimulation CPT 97014/G0283 or mechanical traction CPT 97012) when billed alongside spinal manipulation, or require modifier 59/XU to allow separate payment. Priority Sure LLC’s certified coders ensure every encounter meets payer-specific Local Coverage Determinations (LCDs) to maximize allowable payments and avoid post-payment clawbacks.

Chiropractic Billing FAQs

Does Medicare cover X-rays or physical therapy performed by a chiropractor?

No. Under Medicare Part B, manual manipulation of the spine (CPT 98940–98942) is the only covered service performed by a chiropractor. X-rays, examinations, and physical therapy modalities are statutorily excluded by Medicare, though many commercial plans do cover them.

What happens if the AT modifier is left off a Medicare claim?

If the AT modifier is omitted, Medicare assumes the manipulation is maintenance therapy and automatically denies the claim. Priority Sure’s claim scrubbing ensures the AT modifier is appended whenever active care is documented.

How do you handle personal injury (PI) medical liens and attorney settlements?

Our A/R team maintains dedicated logs for auto accident and PI claims, tracking medical payments coverage (MedPay), Personal Injury Protection (PIP), and communication with patient attorneys to ensure fast settlement payouts upon case resolution.
